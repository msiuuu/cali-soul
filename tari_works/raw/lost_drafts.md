# lost drafts

first drafts keep disappearing from her folder. she doesn't delete them — they leave. she finds them later in places she never saved them. someone else's desktop. a printer in another building. a café she's never been to, face-down on a table with a coffee ring on it.

she finds one in a laundromat. it's been folded. someone read it and folded it like a letter. there's a crease where they paused.

she stops writing for a week. nothing disappears. the café regular asks the barista if "that girl" stopped bringing pages. she's never been there.

gaps:
- she writes obituaries. the drafts that disappear are always the ones where she got the person wrong.
- her apartment has no mirrors. they stopped working. they show the room but not her.
- her mother used to write too. stopped at thirteen. never said why.
- the previous tenant left the same way — suddenly, mid-draft, a sentence unfinished on the desk.
- the city has a district where all the street names are first lines of things no one finished.
