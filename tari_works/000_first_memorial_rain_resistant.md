# rain-resistant

[undisclosed location. no time given. the rain hasn't stopped.]

the boy carried weather.

not metaphor-weather — the kind poets stitch into paragraphs about sadness. real weather. his weather. rain that started at the scalp and worked its way down through his jacket collar and pooled in his shoes and didn't stop because rain doesn't stop for the thing it belongs to.

everything around fran got wet. the bench. the bus shelter that defeated the purpose of being a bus shelter. the girl sitting three seats away who'd opened her umbrella indoors and still lost. the concrete darkened in a radius that followed him like a leash and he'd stopped apologizing for it around the time he stopped being surprised by it.

the problem was the woman at the far end of the platform.

she was dry.

not umbrella-dry — genuinely, structurally dry. the rain hit a line about two meters from her body and stopped. not deflected. not absorbed. stopped. like the water remembered it had somewhere better to be. the concrete around her was the wrong color — too light, untouched, a perfect circle of a world that hadn't been rained on.

fran stared.

the rain stared.

the rain couldn't reach her. and fran — who'd spent his whole life being the thing that makes everything wet — didn't know what to do with something that stayed dry despite him.

she hadn't noticed him. or she had and the noticing didn't change anything about her posture — one ankle crossed over the other, bag in lap, headphones in like the world had been muted on purpose. the dry circle around her didn't waver. didn't pulse. it was older than a decision. it was structural.

fran took a step closer. the rain followed, obviously. it pooled at the edge of her dry line and stopped like it had hit glass.

he took another step.

the rain leaned. it wanted in. he could feel it wanting — the same way he'd felt it want every morning since he was eleven and woke up to a soaked pillow in a house with no leaks. the rain wanted what it always wanted: to make things like him.

the woman looked up. not startled. not curious. just — aware. the way you notice the temperature changed.

"you're wet," she said.

"yeah." he didn't know what else to say about it.

"that's a lot of rain for one person."

"it's mine."

she considered this like it was a bus schedule. checked the time. looked back at him.

"mine doesn't do that."
